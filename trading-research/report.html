Step 2: what each pair does, session by session

Measured over 265 trading days (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026), with sessions in each city's own local time. A fact is marked repeats only if it held within 8 points in both 6-month halves.

XAUUSD

  • Asia's direction matches the whole day's direction 74% of days.
  • London stays inside the Asian box 29% of days (repeats) and almost never breaks both sides (0%).
  • London makes the day's high or low only 16% of the time (repeats). The day's extremes form in Asia (34–43%) or New York.
  • The day closes on the side London broke first 77% of days.
  • New York takes the London high on 65% of days and the London low on 58%.

NAS100

  • New York takes the London high 73% and the London low 68% of days (both repeat). This is the most reliable NAS fact.
  • London rarely sets the day's high or low (16%, repeats).
  • London's first break of the Asian box is upward 59% of the time.
  • After that break it is close to a coin flip: it carries on 54% of the time and reverses 46%.
  • Thursday has the widest range: 520 points, against 475 on average.

GBPUSD

  • London almost always breaks the Asian box: it stays inside only 3% of days (repeats).
  • After London's first break: carries on 55%, reverses 45%. This is the "Judas sweep", and it is a coin flip.
  • London's direction matches the day's direction 63% of the time (repeats).
  • The day closes on the side of London's first break 67% of the time.
  • The day's high is most often set in New York (32%). Wednesday and Thursday are the widest days.

GBPJPY

  • After London's first break of the Asian box, London carries on 64% and reverses 36% (repeats).
  • The day closes on the side of that first break 76% of days (repeats).
  • London's direction matches the day's direction 69% of the time (repeats).
  • London stays inside the Asian box only 10% of days (repeats).
  • Thursday is the widest day: 147 pips, against 134 on average.
Why strong facts didn't become profitable trades. GBPJPY "carries on 64%" sounds like an edge. But a trade with a 1:2 or 1:3 target needs the move to travel 2–3 times its stop within 4 hours, and after costs it usually didn't. A fact about where the day closes is not a fact about where price goes in your 4-hour hold.

Step 8 · XAUUSD: Gold NY Fair-Value-Gap

BEST CANDIDATE, NOT PROVEN Positive in all three periods. Most of the profit came from Oct 2025 – Mar 2026, and the 2025 sealed test was only slightly positive (+0.10R on 16 trades).

  1. 16:00 Doha: read the 1-hour trend.50 EMA on the 1H chart. Up-trend: the last closed 1H candle is above the EMA and the EMA is higher than 5 candles ago. Down-trend: the opposite. No clear trend means no trade.
  2. Find a fair value gap on the 30-minute chart in the trend direction.For an up-trend: three candles where candle 3's low is above candle 1's high, and candle 2 is green. (A down-trend is the mirror.) Candle 3 must close between 16:00 and 18:00 Doha.
  3. Place a limit order at the middle of the gap.Stop just beyond candle 1's far end. Target 1:3. Cancel the order if it hasn't filled within 1 hour, or by 18:00. If two gaps appear, keep only the order that fills first.
  4. Size for 0.5% risk.Lots = (account × 0.5%) ÷ (stop distance in $ × 100 oz). Check your broker's contract size.
  5. From your phone: close the trade 4 hours after the fill if the stop or target hasn't closed it already.

Step 8 · GBPJPY: VWAP Pullback

NOT SAFE FOR A 5% LIMIT +6.6% over 18 months, but a −14.5% drawdown, and it lost money in the 2025 sealed test.

  1. Put VWAP on the 15-minute chart, anchored at 03:00 Doha (the start of the trading day).
  2. Between 09:00 and 12:00 Doha, read the trend:at least 4 of the last 6 fifteen-minute candles closed on the same side of VWAP.
  3. Entry:a candle dips back to VWAP and closes back on the trend side, in the trend direction (a green candle closing above VWAP for a buy). Enter at the close.
  4. Stop beyond the lowest low (or highest high) of the last 6 candles. Target 1:3. Close after 4 hours.

Step 8 · GBPUSD: Breakout + Retest

NO EDGE −4.3% over 18 months, and it lost in 2 of 3 periods. This was the best of 167 GBPUSD versions; everything else was worse.

  1. Mark the Asian box (03:00–09:00 Doha high and low).

  3. Place a limit order back at the box edge (the retest). Stop beyond the breakout candle. Target 1:3. Cancel at 12:00 if not filled. Close after 4 hours.

The useful NAS fact still stands: New York takes the London high or low on about 70% of days. No entry rule I tested turned that into a profit within 4 hours after costs.

How it was tested

  1. Data: 1-minute Dukascopy prices, Apr 2025 – Oct 2026, for all four pairs: about 2 million candles.
  2. Strict accounting: spread and commission on every trade. Buy limits fill only when the ask reaches them, and short trades exit on the ask. A stop and target hit in the same minute count as a loss. Entries only in your windows. A 4-hour maximum hold.
  3. Selection on Oct 2025 – Oct 2026: a version had to be profitable in both 6-month halves.
  4. Sealed test 1 (Oct 2025 – Mar 2026), used in version 1, and sealed test 2 (Apr–Sep 2025). Rules were written down and locked before each test.
  5. Checkers: the Reality Checker rebuilt and hand-checked trades twice and found 6 issues, all fixed. One of them made limit-order strategies look better than they were. The Statistician measured how much of each result could be luck.

Limits of this research

  • Only the rule types listed above were tested. Discretionary reading (judging context by eye) can't be backtested this way.
  • Prices come from one data feed. Your broker's prices and spreads will differ slightly.
  • The news-day precautions are sensible, but they weren't part of the tests.
  • 18 months covers several market moods, but the future can differ again. Re-check every 30 live trades.
