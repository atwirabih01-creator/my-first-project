The big lesson: the popular idea that "London sweeps the Asian high/low and then reverses" happens only about 41–55% of the time. On its own that is a coin flip, and every version of it I tested failed. What did repeat in both halves of the data was the opposite on gold: once London breaks the Asian box in the direction of the 1-hour trend, it tends to keep going for the next few hours.

How it was tested

  1. Data: 1-minute prices for all four markets from Dukascopy, Oct 2025 – Oct 2026 (about 1.4 million candles). Every trade was replayed minute by minute.
  2. Strict accounting: spread and commission are subtracted from every trade. If the stop and the target are hit in the same minute, it counts as a loss. Entries are allowed only inside your two windows. Every trade closes after 4 hours at most.
  3. Round 1: five well-known strategies (Asian sweep reversal, London breakout, New York opening-range breakout, trend pullback, previous-day/London high-low sweep reversal) on 5, 15, 30 and 60-minute charts, with 1:2 and 1:3 targets. About 100 versions each for GBPUSD, gold and GBPJPY, picked on Apr–mid-Jul and checked on mid-Jul–Oct. Most failed the second half.
  4. Round 2: rules built from behaviour that repeated in both halves, about 130 versions per market.
  5. Sealed exam: the final four rules were written down and locked, then tested once on Oct 2025 – Mar 2026, data that had never been looked at. That period is mostly winter, which is the season you are about to trade.
  6. Two independent checkers. A Reality Checker rebuilt every one of the 227 trades and checked 20 by hand against the raw prices. It found no "peeking into the future" mistakes, and the two small bugs it found were fixed. A Statistician measured how much of each result could be luck, given how many rules were tried, and correctly predicted that GBPUSD and GBPJPY would fail.

The strategy: Gold London Breakout

Everything in Doha time. Three things on your chart: the Asian box, a 50 EMA on the 1-hour chart, and the 30-minute chart. No other indicators.

Before 09:00: prepare (5 minutes)

  1. Draw the Asian box.On gold, mark the highest high and the lowest low from 03:00 to 09:00 Doha. Draw a line at the exact middle. That middle line is your stop.
  2. Read the 1-hour trend.On the 1-hour chart, add a 50-period EMA. Up-trend: the last closed 1H candle is above the EMA and the EMA is higher than it was 5 candles ago. Down-trend: below the EMA and the EMA is lower than 5 candles ago. Anything else is no trade today.
  3. Check the box isn't too big.Stop distance = box top (or bottom) minus the middle line. If the stop is bigger than 60% of gold's average daily range (about $60 recently), skip the day. Typical stop: about $28.

09:00–12:00: the entry

  1. Watch the 30-minute candles close.Closes happen at 09:30, 10:00, 10:30 … 12:00. Only the first 30-minute candle that closes outside the box counts. A wick through the box is not a signal.
  2. Up-trend + first close above the box → BUY at that candle's close.
    Down-trend + first close below the box → SELL at that candle's close.
  3. If the first close outside the box goes against the trend, there is no trade today.Don't wait for a second breakout the other way. This filter roughly doubles the result.
  4. If nothing closes outside the box by 12:00, there is no trade today.Between the trend filter and this rule, you'll have no trade on about two days out of three. No trade is a valid result.

Placing the order

  1. Stop loss at the box middle line.
  2. Take profit at 3× the stop distance (1:3).Be realistic: it was reached on only 4 of 88 trades. It is there to catch the big days.
  3. Position size for 1% risk.Lots = (account × 1%) ÷ (stop in $ × 100), for a 100-ounce lot (check your broker's contract size). Example: $100,000 account, $28 stop → $1,000 ÷ $2,800 = 0.36 lots. On a $10,000 account: 0.04 lots.
  4. Set a phone alarm for exactly 4 hours after entry.

After 12:00: from your phone

  1. Do nothing until the alarm.Don't move the stop to breakeven: I tested that, and it slightly lowered the result. Don't close early because it "looks weak".
  2. At the alarm, close the trade at market, win or lose, if the stop or target hasn't already closed it.86% of trades ended this way. The 4-hour hold is where the profit comes from. Entry 10:00 means close at 14:00.

Confluence, confirmation, and when to step back

Full confidence = all of these true

  • 1H trend clearly defined (price and EMA slope agree).
  • The first 30-minute close outside the box is in the trend direction.
  • Stop is within the size limit ($60 or less recently).
  • Entry candle closes between 09:30 and 12:00.
  • No top-tier US release (CPI, jobs report) during your 4-hour hold.

There is no "extra-confident" version. Adding more filters made the results look better on past data but less reliable. When every box is ticked, take the trade at full size.

Step back when

  • The trend is unclear or the first breakout goes against it: no trade.
  • A top-tier US release (CPI, jobs, Fed) falls inside your hold: close 5 minutes before it, or skip entries after 11:00. Precaution, not tested.
  • Your account is down 3% from its peak: cut risk to 0.5% until a new high.
  • 6 losses in a row (worse than anything in 12 months): stop and review.
  • Christmas–New Year week and thin holiday sessions.

What to avoid

  • Fading the Asian sweep on gold ("it took the high, so I'll sell"). It was tested in every form and does not pay.
  • Entering on a wick or before the 30-minute candle closes.
  • Adding GBPUSD, GBPJPY or NAS100 trades to hit "more green days". In the sealed test that combination failed a 5% limit.
  • Moving the stop further away, or holding past 4 hours "to give it room".
  • Raising risk after a win streak.

NAS100: on watch, not for the challenge

The rule (New York open fade): between 16:00 and 18:00 Doha, on the 5-minute chart, check whether NAS100 has moved at least 30% of its average daily range in the last 3 hours. If it has, wait for a 5-minute candle that closes against that move and beyond the previous candle's low (or high). Enter at the close. Stop beyond the last hour's extreme, target 1:2, close after 4 hours.

Why it's on hold: from March to September it averaged +0.5R per trade and made money every month. From November to February it was flat or losing. I checked whether the winter New York clock change explained it, and it didn't: moving the window made winter worse. An edge that switches off without a known reason doesn't belong in a challenge account. Track it on demo this winter. If it is still positive by mid-March 2027 (the next US clock change), consider it at 0.5% risk.

GBPUSD and GBPJPY: no edge found

About 240 rule versions were tested on each, and the best ones failed the sealed exam:

  • GBPUSD: the London breakout with the trend averaged +0.015R over 12 months, so trading costs eat the whole edge. The Statistician found its earlier profit came from just 3–4 lucky trades.
  • GBPJPY: its best rule averaged −0.09R over 12 months and lost 11.5R in the sealed exam.
  • The behaviour study above still helps you read them. Both break their Asian box almost every day (93% and 85%), but with no reliable follow-through or reversal you can bank on.

If you want to keep trying them, use demo only, never the challenge.

Limits of this research

  • No strategy can promise no losing trades. This one loses on about 47% of trades and had a run of 5 losses. The protection comes from the risk rules, not from predicting every move.
  • 12 months is a modest sample: 88 gold trades. The Statistician's estimate of the real long-run edge is roughly +0.1 to +0.3R per trade, so expect a slower path than the best backtest weeks.
  • The edge was weaker in Nov–Feb (about +0.04R in those months) than in spring and summer. Be patient through winter.
  • Prices come from one data feed (Dukascopy). Your prop broker's gold quotes and spreads will differ slightly. Costs were modelled at $0.50 per ounce, round trip.
  • The news-day precautions above are sensible but were not part of the test.
  • Past behaviour can change. Re-check after every 30 live trades.
